What’s new — features

New tools for the 2026-27 season.

  • NewUnsigned free agents are out. A skater with no contract for 2026-27 no longer carries a card or a rank. Their old club stays next to their name in every data source until they sign, which made them look rostered when they aren’t. Each one comes back with a full projection the day he signs somewhere.
  • NewCopy sits beside Export CSV. The whole board goes to the clipboard tab separated, so it pastes straight into Google Sheets or Excel with the columns already split. It’s quicker than a download for most people, and it’s the one that still works on a locked-down work network, where a new site’s downloads are often blocked.
  • NewShooting % is a category, read off the goals and shots already projected beside it. Both percentage columns go blank for anyone without the volume to earn one, because a winger who takes 4 draws and wins 3 is not a 75% faceoff man.
  • NewDraft Tools says up front what it scores: all 17 skater categories by name, and beside them what we leave alone, which is goalies, salary and auction values, keeper values and game-winning goals.
  • NewEvery category is a column on the board, with the projected number in it and green shading on the best of them, so you can see who gives you blocks without opening a card. Points is always there, whether your league counts it or not. Click a header to sort on it, drag one to move it. Names and clubs link out to the card and the depth chart.
  • NewYour league is remembered — settings, categories, roster and the column order you dragged into place all keep in this browser, so the board opens the way you left it.
  • NewEvery card now says how replaceable a player is: the makeup box opens with a rarity score, built from the same comparison the match percentages beneath it already use. Tom Wilson’s closest match in the league is an 83% fit and Tanner Jeannot’s is 97%, and until now both cards just showed 4 comps in a box.
  • NewMedals — scarcity medals mark the category combinations almost nobody holds, each stamped with its holder count, because a bar per category can never show that elite assists plus elite hits has 1 holder in the league (Brady Tkachuk). Under them sit trait medals for what the rate history says a player does: Fight Card, Hit Machine, Net-Front, Faceoff Boss, Kill Regular, plus 4 that track what climbed through last season, including power-play promotions. Every one carries his own number, so Zach Hyman’s Net-Front medal reads 243 high-danger chances per 82.
  • NewLinks to the site now open into a preview card — paste a player’s page into a text, Slack or Discord and it unfurls into his card: his name, his club colour, and the shape of his category profile. That shape comes from where he ranks rather than his raw totals, so a card you posted in August still reads true after the projection behind it moves.
  • NewCustom Draft Tools is opening soon — the full ranked board, every skater scored for your own league’s categories and roster settings, and the whole projection set as a CSV. Compare has moved in with it. Player cards, Lines, the Regression Explorer and the Schedule stay free, and every card still shows that player’s own ranks.
  • NewA Players page — browse every projected skater by club, see the current risers and downers, and check which cards were hand-reviewed most recently.
  • NewEvery card now opens with a square built to be posted — the top of a player page is a self-contained 1:1 card: his category radar, his positional rank beside his overall rank, the 3 scores, his club colour behind it, and the handle. Screenshot it and everything that explains the number travels with it. The full card sits underneath with everything it always had.
  • NewA jump list beside the square — a player page runs long, so the top of every card now lists what is on it: the overview, the highlighted metrics, similar players, deployment, and each of the 4 category boxes. Click a line and the page goes there. A player who gets no highlights section gets no link to one.
  • NewMetrics worth highlighting — every card now opens with the 4 charts that actually explain its projection, picked automatically from the 40 or so further down the page. They surface where a player broke from his own 3-year rate, where his season split in half, where a rate sits at the edge of the league, and where we’re taking the number somewhere other than last year. Each caption is measured off the chart drawn beside it, so nothing gets claimed that the picture doesn’t show. Players with almost no ice time last season get no section at all, which is the honest answer for them.
  • NewEvery stat name on a card is a link now — click G, Hits, PPP or PP TOI in the projection table, or any label on the banner, and the card jumps to the section that explains that number. The table also shows H1 and H2 columns for 2025-26, so you can see whether a line was carried by one half of the season or held all year.
  • NewProjection Report Card — we graded our 2025-26 preseason projections head-to-head against 10 other public projectors on draft-day usefulness and category accuracy, then put our own misses on the same table as everyone else’s — including the deeper players below our published board, so nothing is cherry-picked. Open the Report Card →

  • NewPlayer bios on every card — birthplace country, height and weight, and full draft info (year, overall pick, and team) now sit under each player’s name.
  • NewHand-review status on the Lines page — each club’s depth chart is now marked Reviewed or Auto as we work through all 32 teams alphabetically, checking lines, deployment, and player projections against real usage. Open the Lines page →

What’s new — players

Notable projection calls — search any player up top for the full card.

  • HedgeYegor Chinakhov — power-play time to 2.40, which is the role he actually finished last season in. He is the leading candidate for Pittsburgh’s open top-unit seat, and paying him above his own finish was pricing a promotion nobody has given him. Malkin goes back up to 3.05.
  • BumpSidney Crosby — ice time to 19.6 a night, and 84 points with it. His scoring rate has not shifted in 4 seasons, holding between 3.35 and 3.42 per 60, and we had him under his own minutes floor on top of that.
  • BumpBryan Rust — assists to 39. His rate has been flat for 3 straight seasons at 2.77, 2.78 and 2.72 per 60 on close to 20 minutes a night, and our line paid 2.39.
  • BumpChandler Stephenson — ice time to 19.5. His minutes are the steadiest thing about him, 19.02, 18.48, 19.51 and 19.38 over 4 seasons, and we had him below the lowest. The scoring rate stays faded, because that slide is real.
  • BumpMathew Barzal — assists to 59 on the move to 2C. Away from Horvat he starts more scoring sequences instead of losing the finish to a feed from the blue line.
