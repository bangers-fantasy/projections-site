What’s new — features

New tools for the 2026-27 season.

  • NewCompare plots everyone you picked on one chart. It’s the quadrant from the player card, offense up the side and peripherals across, with every skater in the pool behind them as a grey dot. The dashed lines cut the top quarter of each half, so you can see which of your candidates is a scorer, which is a banger and which is neither. Forwards and defensemen get their own backdrop, and the switch sits above the chart. Open Compare →
  • FixedThe score at the top of a card now matches the rankings. It was measured against the median player at the position while every other page measured against a waiver-wire replacement, so Drake Batherson’s card headline read 59 while the ranking under it read 81. The gap also changed by position, about 22 points for a forward and 5 for a defenceman, which distorted every forward-against-defenceman comparison. The tiles print the ranking’s number now, the 2 halves still add to the total, and a new publish check compares every card’s score against the rankings so they can’t drift apart again.
  • ImprovedMatthew Knies and Drake Batherson both move up. Knies played through a knee injury for most of last season, and his 2 halves barely look like the same player: a 181-shot, 203-hit pace per 82 over the first half, 112 and 115 over the second. His line now sits between the halves and closer to the healthy one, 70 points on 169 shots and 185 hits. Batherson moves on the 2 categories his own record already backed. His assist pace has been flat at 40, 38, 42 and 39 across 4 full seasons and his power-play pace has run near 31 per 82, so 43 assists and 30 power-play points replace 41 and 28. His goals stay at 27, because last season’s 34-goal pace came on a shooting-percentage spike while his shot rate fell. See the Movers page →
  • FixedThe age on a card is the player’s age today: it used to be season age, measured at February 1, which read a year high for 42% of the roster. Connor McDavid is 29 and his card said 30.1, and Gavin McKenna is 18 and his said 19.1. The model still prices a player at the age he’ll play most of the season at, because that’s the right input for a projection and it doesn’t drift between builds, but the number you read is now just his age.
  • Fixed111 prospects had no date of birth at all, and now they do. The bio data only covers players who have taken an NHL shift, and a third of these have never played a game, so their age had been falling back to an integer taken in early July. That ran a full year young on 58 of them, on the one input the model uses to decide who is still developing. The dates come from the NHL’s own player records. 11 projections moved, nearly all of them down.
  • Improved6 players moved on a second look at the whole board, and the restraint is the point. Most of what looked like a gap turned out to be a position we had already argued out and deliberately stopped short on, so those were left alone and only the untouched ones were opened. Adam Klapka comes down on goals, shots and blocks together, because his one full season contradicts the rates a 31-game sample had bought him. Alex Steeves drops from 17 goals to 13, off 16.3% shooting across 43 games. Thomas Harley goes the other way, 6 power-play points up to 9, since his power-play time finished last season climbing rather than falling. See the Movers page →
  • FixedCustom Rankings and the Draft Board work on a phone again: both tools carry their own stylesheet, and the site rewrites it on the way in so it can’t leak onto the rest of the pages. That rewrite was dropping every phone rule in it, so a 390px screen got the desktop layout, a 308px settings column with about 80px left over for the board. The phone rules survive the rewrite now, so the settings stack above the board and the table gets the full width. Tier lines on the board moved up a row in the same pass, so a tier opens directly under its own line instead of under its second player.
  • Improved16 players moved on a sweep of the offseason notes, and every proposal had to survive a second look before it was applied. That step killed half the shortlist: where our line already sat where it should, the case for moving it failed, and 11 players were left alone. Tom Wilson’s goals come down from 30 to 28, since our line still implied he shoots 17.5% after 2 straight seasons near 19.5%. Josh Morrissey comes down to 59 points after closing last year with 13 points in his final 28 games. Michael Misa goes the other way, 42 points up to 46 on a top-6 role. See the Movers page →
  • ImprovedEvery category on the board re-examined player by player, and 87 players moved. The largest corrections sat on the power play and on defencemen’s peripherals. Simon Nemec’s assists go from 18 to 23. Seth Jones moves on 4 categories at once, up on assists and power-play points and down on hits and blocks, where his own last season backs the lower number. Owen Power’s assists come down from 36 to 31. Nobody is moved the full distance, and where our line is backed by a player’s own second half the move stays small: Connor McDavid comes down 2 percent and no further. See the Movers page →
  • ImprovedDetroit reviewed, and the board is complete — the Red Wings were the one club held back while Dylan Larkin’s trade request sat unresolved, and they’re now checked line by line like the other 31. He’s still projected in Detroit, since nothing has happened yet, and his card says so plainly. Around him, Lucas Raymond’s minutes and his scoring both go up, Viktor Arvidsson gets the even-strength time to match the power-play spot he inherits, Moritz Seider’s power-play minutes come down to what he’s actually run, and the hit and penalty-minute lines are rebuilt across the roster. All 32 clubs are now hand-reviewed. See the Lines page →
  • FixedThe 3 scores at the top of a card add up again: offense is goals, assists and power-play points, peripherals is everything else, and the 2 are supposed to sum to the overall score. They were briefly printed as percentiles, which fixed an unreadable scale and broke that: Brandon Hagel’s offense and peripherals contribute almost the same amount to his score, 57 points against 56, and they showed as 91 and 82. All 3 are back to score points, counted from a median skater at the same position rather than from a waiver-wire baseline, so a typical forward reads 0 and Hagel reads 64 plus 27 for 91. The number under each one is what that half of his game is worth.
  • FixedEvery bar on a card now fills against the top of the league: they used to fill by rank, which squashed the top of every category together. 40 goals and 50 goals are the 96th and 99th best, so an elite scorer and the league leader drew almost the same bar while the counts beside them were 25% apart, and Brandon Hagel’s 52 hits drew a third of a bar. A full bar is now the 5th-best value at that position, so 44 goals fills a forward’s bar and 19 fills a defenceman’s, 314 hits fills a forward’s and 237 a defenceman’s. Hagel’s 52 hits draw 17%.
  • NewMontreal’s lines are reviewed: the top power-play unit came back the same in every read we have, which is rare. The defence pairs changed the most. Our raw ice time had Hutson and Dobson together on the top pair, and both of them belong on separate pairs, which is what the chart shows now.
  • FixedA note about one player was showing up on 2 of his linemates: a read about Juraj Slafkovsky’s 80-point case was attached to Nick Suzuki and Cole Caufield as well, because all 3 were named in the same sentence. Suzuki comes down 2 points. Noah Dobson’s card also carried a read about keeping the Islanders secure, which is his old team.
  • ImprovedAgeing costs something from 30 now, not 33: the model charged nothing at all for the step out of a player’s peak years until 33 for forwards and 35 for defencemen. Measured across the wider field, our 30-and-over skaters ran 3 to 5% high across the board while every younger group sat at or below them. All of them are inside 2% now.
  • FixedA 5-game quarter is no longer read as how a season ended: the model leans on the last quarter of the year to work out what a role has become, and it was doing that off any quarter with games in it. Tyler Seguin’s year ended in January, so his final 6 games were standing in as his finish. Quarters under 8 games are ignored now, and a thin one counts for less.
  • FixedFlorida’s shot totals: 5-on-5 shot rates fell about 10% league wide last season and Florida fell twice as far, which left the model reverting the whole roster back toward the old counting basis. Panthers shots ran 9% above where they should have landed, twice the lean of any other club.
  • FixedFaceoff % was printing the wrong number: the column showed a player’s wins minus his losses under a header with a percent sign on it, so J.T. Miller read as 227%. It shows the percentage now. Faceoff losses is gone as a category of its own, since nobody counts losses without counting wins.
  • NewShooting % is a category, read off the goals and shots already projected beside it. Both percentage columns go blank for anyone without the volume to earn one, because a winger who takes 4 draws and wins 3 is not a 75% faceoff man.
  • ImprovedIce time is per game and reads as 27:59, in place of a per-82 total of 1,829 minutes. It’s also the one column that stays put when you switch to the projected-games basis, since playing 60 games doesn’t shorten anyone’s shifts.
  • ImprovedPlus/minus and shorthanded points, tested against 4 seasons of results. Plus/minus repeats at r = 0.34 season over season, so the projection now regresses by the amount that testing says it should, and it reads even-strength ice time in place of 5-on-5. Both columns are deliberately compressed: the top 10 in on-ice goal differential averaged +36 over the last 3 seasons and came back at +17.
  • NewDraft Tools says up front what it scores: all 17 skater categories by name, and beside them what we leave alone, which is goalies, salary and auction values, keeper values and game-winning goals.
  • ImprovedSearch sits in the top bar: it had a strip of its own, which meant 2 rows of header stuck to the top of the screen before the page started. It shares the line with the logo and the menu now, and drops to its own row on a phone.
  • ImprovedThe draft tools are real pages now: Custom Rankings and the Draft Board used to open in a box with its own scrollbar, so the page underneath sat frozen while you scrolled the board. They scroll like every other page, and the league settings stay beside you as you go down the list.
  • ImprovedThe rankings board carries every skater: it opens on the top 150 and takes 50 more at a time, or all 876 at once. Everyone was always scored; only the first 150 used to be reachable.
  • NewEvery category is a column on the board, with the projected number in it and green shading on the best of them, so you can see who gives you blocks without opening a card. Points is always there, whether your league counts it or not. Click a header to sort on it, drag one to move it. Names and clubs link out to the card and the depth chart.
  • NewYour league is remembered — settings, categories, roster and the column order you dragged into place all keep in this browser, so the board opens the way you left it.

  • ImprovedCard bars and radars are shaded against a deeper pool: the top 350 forwards and top 180 defencemen, each ranked at his own position, in place of the top 250 skaters overall. The old pool held about 15 forwards a club, so ordinary good seasons shaded below the median. A 25-goal year now reads as the solid forward season it is.
  • ImprovedThe menu moved behind one button: the nav had grown to 9 links, which on a phone meant 3 rows of header before the page even started. It’s the same menu everywhere now, free or paid.
  • ImprovedThe report card is a real page on the site: it was built separately and copied in, so it had none of the search bar, the dark-mode switch or the footer. It’s built the same way as every other page now.
  • NewEvery card now says how replaceable a player is: the makeup box opens with a rarity score, built from the same comparison the match percentages beneath it already use. Tom Wilson’s closest match in the league is an 83% fit and Tanner Jeannot’s is 97%, and until now both cards just showed 4 comps in a box.
  • NewMedals — scarcity medals mark the category combinations almost nobody holds, each stamped with its holder count, because a bar per category can never show that elite assists plus elite hits has 1 holder in the league (Brady Tkachuk). Under them sit trait medals for what the rate history says a player does: Fight Card, Hit Machine, Net-Front, Faceoff Boss, Kill Regular, plus 4 that track what climbed through last season, including power-play promotions. Every one carries his own number, so Zach Hyman’s Net-Front medal reads 243 high-danger chances per 82.

That shape comes from where he ranks rather than his raw totals, so a card you posted in August still reads true after the projection behind it moves.

  • NewThe banger score’s weights are now measured instead of hand-set — the 7 multipliers behind every score used to be judgment calls. They are now priced by a rule: a weight is a price per unit, and units are cheap in a category where everyone piles up a lot of them. Hits run from about 20 to 300 across rosterable skaters, so one hit is a sliver of that range; goals run from about 10 to 50, so one goal is a big slice of it. The rule reproduces the old hand-set weights with an r² of 0.968, which is what earned it the job, and it prices shots and blocks higher than the old numbers did while easing off assists and power-play points. Scores sit on the same scale as before and the top of the board barely moves, but shot-blocking defencemen climb: Jacob Trouba, Darnell Nurse and MacKenzie Weegar all gain 25 spots or more.

Each caption is measured off the chart drawn beside it, so nothing gets claimed that the picture doesn’t show. Players with almost no ice time last season get no section at all, which is the honest answer for them.

  • FixA power-play cliff that decided points by a rounding error — when we project a player’s power-play scoring rate we normally pull it partway back toward his last 3 years, because one season on the power play is a small, noisy sample. We used to switch that off completely for anyone whose projected power-play minutes rose more than 15%, which fits a player promoted from nothing to the top unit but not an established one whose minutes merely edged up. The cutoff also landed mid-range: Mitch Marner came out at 15.26% and lost 6 points for it, with 29 other skaters sitting within a whisker of the same line. That pullback now fades in gradually instead of snapping off, so a genuine promotion is still respected and a small bump no longer throws away 3 years of history. 60 players moved, up and down, most by a point or so.
  • ImprovedSecond pass on the first six teams — Anaheim, Boston, Buffalo, Calgary, Chicago and Columbus have now been re-checked category by category against two independent sets of numbers, not just our own model.   • ImprovedWe tested one of our own rules — when a player’s shot or block rate has moved the same direction for several years, how much should we trust the trend versus his longer track record? We checked both against what actually happened last season. Blocks genuinely bounce around, so leaning on the longer record is right there — only about a quarter of block-rate trends continued. Shots are far more repeatable and want the most recent season, which is already how our model treats them. Worth saying plainly: one of our own Columbus corrections was on the wrong side of this, and we reverted it the same day.
  • ImprovedHits and blocks for young players — for a prospect we blend a scouting projection with whatever he did in the NHL. We were applying that blend to hits and blocks too, which quietly halved them. Those are the steadiest things a young player does and scouting reports say little about them, so they now lean on what the player actually did.
  • ImprovedA few good games no longer set a player’s physical stats — a hit or block rate from a short stint used to carry across a full season at full strength. It now gets weighted by how much of the season there actually was. A player whose rate is steady year after year is left alone; only the ones built on a handful of games move.
  • FixOffseason moves that our roster feed missed — a player who signs or gets traded after the roster data is pulled can stay listed with his old club, which quietly keeps him off his new team’s depth chart. What’s new — players

Notable projection calls — search any player up top for the full card.

  • FadeTom Wilson — goals from 30 to 28. He shot 19.5% and 19.6% in back-to-back seasons and our line still implied 17.5%, a rate almost nobody holds for a 3rd year. His hits and penalty minutes are untouched.
  • BumpLuke Hughes — power-play minutes to 2.85 and assists to 47. The blue line thinned again over the summer and the other half of the rotation saw his power-play share fall from 67.4% to 50%, so the minutes were the part still lagging behind the role.
  • FadeGavin McKenna — 62 points, down from 70. A rookie season that size has happened twice in 20 years, and the assists were the column running ahead of everything else.
  • BumpSimon Nemec — assists to 23 and power-play points to 9. This was the widest correction on the whole board, up off our 18 assists. It stops at 23 because his own second half paced 9 assists after a first half at 29.
  • FadeOwen Power — assists trimmed from 36 to 31. His own last season paced 21 with a second half at 25, and the second-unit quarterback job is what keeps him above that number.
  • BumpAaron Ekblad — hits to 99 and blocks to 100. His 3-year paces are 118 and 104, well above our line in both, so the projection was pricing the second half of last season and little else.
  • FadeOliver Kapanen — trimmed to 34 points. He scored 22 goals while shooting 17.8% at 5-on-5, a third more than his chances called for, and with only 18 earlier NHL games behind him the model had no multi-year mark to pull that back toward. His shooting went 26.1, 11.8, 18.2 and 17.6 by quarter.
  • FadeIvan Demidov — trimmed to 68 points, assists only. His rookie helpers came with a 14.5% on-ice shooting percentage at 5-on-5, half again the league median. The 24 goals and the top power-play minutes stay, since his power-play time climbed from 2.5 to 3.6 a night across the year.
  • BumpCole Caufield — raised to 77 points. His hits were projected at 43 when his last 3 seasons are 51, 65 and 56, and his assists sat at the bottom of his own range. Goals hold at 41, because the 51 he scored came on a 19.8% shooting percentage.
  • BumpKaiden Guhle — hits to 170 and penalty minutes to 78. His penalties have climbed every season from 50 to 86, and his hits set a career high last year at 181. Both were projected under his own last 2 years.
  • FixedBrett Berard — rebuilt from the ground up. His 13 games last season fell under the sample floor, so the model fell back on his 2024-25 line and held it flat, which produced a projection identical to that season in every single category.
  • FadeMoritz Seider — trimmed to 57 points. We were carrying a manual assist boost on top of a career year the model already anchors on, which is the same season counted twice, and it had him at 54 assists when his best is 50. The top power-play job and the peripherals are untouched.
  • BumpSeth Jones — raised to 45 points. the range is 41 to 43 assists against our 32, and our fade leaned partly on a power-play dip read off 12 games he played coming back from injury. The secondary assists and the falling shot volume still cost him something, so this only closes part of the gap.
  • BumpViktor Arvidsson — raised to 55 points. Patrick Kane’s power-play spot is open and Arvidsson is the obvious fill, which the model can’t see from last season. He has done this before, with 25 power-play points in 2022-23.
  • FadeTyler Seguin — trimmed to 55 points. He turns 35 coming off a year that ended in January, and the 27 games before it ran a 31.6% shooting percentage and a 65% point share. Our projected assist rate had ended up above both last season and his 3-year mark.
  • FadeWyatt Johnston — hits cut to 58. One hot quarter had him projected for 77, which is 37% above anything he has recorded in 4 seasons. His scoring is unchanged.
