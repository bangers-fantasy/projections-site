How to read this card

The 3 scores at the top are that same score, split into offense and peripherals, which add to the total. They match the rankings exactly. The bar fill and the radar axes are percentiles, both among the top 179 defensemen by banger rank; the number beside each category bar is the projected count. The radar’s dashed ring is the median. The quadrant plots both halves of the score in points. The crosshair marks the top quarter of each, and the shaded band runs from the median up to it. A corner name needs both halves clear of that band, so nobody gets called all scoring and no grit on median grit.

Context-adjusted (▲/▼ vs the rate-model baseline): Hits corrected. The model read a late-season hit surge as a role change and projected 82, above 3 of his 4 seasons. That surge came in an 18-game final quarter where his scoring fell to 0.78 points per 60, his worst of the year. Shots were cut from 178 to 164 in Aug 2026, and the reason is worth stating: his power-play time halved when he arrived in Montreal, from 3.01 a night to 1.52, and it stayed between 1.40 and 1.62 in every quarter of the season, because Hutson runs the top unit there. He shoots 12.81 per 60 on the power play against 5.04 at even strength, so those lost minutes cost shots at 2.5 times his ordinary rate. Our projected ice time already carried that demotion, but the shot rate applied to it was still blended from 3 Islanders seasons. Built from his own rates at his own projected minutes, the honest number is 160. Blocks were raised from 159 to 168 at the same time: holding them flat was right in principle, since blocks revert instead of trending, but the anchor was a 4-year average of totals that included a season at 20.38 minutes a night. Averaging his rate and applying our own projected minutes is the cleaner read. Scoring is unchanged at 46.
